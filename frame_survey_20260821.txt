1. WHAT IS ACTUALLY WRONG
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
a) RIGHT Y RAIL LOW AT REAR - shimmable, and the only mechanical fault worth
   correcting. Rail differential front-to-rear:
        cold 23C   0.344 mm      45C   0.372 mm      60C   0.358 mm
   TEMPERATURE INDEPENDENT to +/-0.02mm across 23K. Measure it cold.
   -> shim schedule, section 3.

b) X BEAM BOWS WITH TEMPERATURE - not correctable, mesh it.
   -11.7 microns/K, LINEAR in frame dT (verified at 3 temperatures: the change
   at 45C is 0.597 of the total to 60C, where linear predicts 0.571).
   Cause: steel MGN9 rail on a 20x20x3 6063 beam behaves as a bimetallic strip.
   A shim is a constant and this is not - it cannot be corrected mechanically.
   The real fix is the dual MGN9 sandwich already on the project list: symmetry
   cancels the couple, and it also addresses the 215Hz torsional mode.

c) Z DATUM MOVES A LOT WITH TEMPERATURE.
        cold -> 45C   +0.511 mm       cold -> 60C   +0.782 mm
   Linear in chamber dT to ~1%. Harmless IF you re-datum hot; fatal if you home
   cold and print hot. Print start MUST re-datum once soaked.

d) THE BED IS NOT THE PROBLEM. It rests freely on three points, so it can only
   contribute a PLANE, and G32 nulls a plane at every print. Its mounting needs
   no work.

e) THE FRAME NEEDS NO LEVELLING. 4.35mm of shim under one foot moved the twist
   by 6 microns (0.0014 mm per mm). Machine position on the bench is not a
   variable. Do not buy levelling feet or a stand.

f) SURFACE ERRORS, for information - the mesh handles these, no action:
        granite  0.114 mm astigmatic (it is NOT a flat reference)
        FR4      0.028 mm astigmatic
   The FR4's warp happens to oppose the granite's and cancels much of it. Its
   CURRENT ORIENTATION IS THE GOOD ONE - 0.395mm range vs 0.635mm rotated 90.
   Mark it before ever lifting it.

g) REPEATABILITY: 4.7 microns cold, 8-10 microns hot, rms between back-to-back
   maps. The machine measures far better than it is built.

2. CAUSE FOUND - READ BEFORE CUTTING SHIMS
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
RESOLVED 22/08/2026: the twist term is sensitive to Y RAIL FASTENER TORQUE.
The twist CHANGED OVERNIGHT 21->22/08 with the machine idle: 0.040 -> 0.122mm
(machine-only figures, surfaces removed). Every other candidate was ruled out
by measurement: bed mount (plane only, can't twist), bench/support (would need
68mm of bench error), granite orientation (0.017mm), frame temperature (too
small), umbilical (Key-Bak is 4oz = 1.11N = 4 microns, and a toolhead force
makes a BOWL not a twist). Belts were not touched.
This was flagged as a candidate at the time and not confirmed ("screws were
tightened on the machine that night - input shaping work"). Confirmed later
22/08/2026: Trev slackened and retightened the Y rail screws for an input-
shaping session ("had bollocked them up way too tight"). A same-day cold 4x4
mesh taken straight afterwards moved by ~50 microns (corner-twist metric 0.339
-> 0.287mm) relative to the mesh taken before that retightening - same
mechanism, same direction of effect, caught in the act this time.
This is the SAME kind of coupling as the X beam bimetallic bow (section on the
X beam, steel rail bolted to aluminium extrusion) - the rail clamps to the
frame member and the member deflects with clamping state - except the driving
variable here is FASTENER TORQUE, not temperature. Retorque the rail and the
twist moves, by tens of microns per event, comparable to a meaningful fraction
of the shim schedule itself.
CONSEQUENCE: the shim schedule below is only as good as the fastener torque it
was measured at. It is NOT a fixed machine constant like the X beam bow or the
Z-datum thermal shift - it is a function of how tight these screws currently
are. Before cutting or fitting shims:
  1. Do not touch the Y rail fasteners between the reference mesh and fitting.
  2. If they must come out (to insert shims, they will), retighten to the same,
     repeatable spec every time - a torque wrench and a recorded torque value,
     not "by feel". Whatever value is chosen, use it for the final cold
     reference mesh AND for final assembly, so the schedule and the built
     machine match.
  3. Re-measure the cold twist immediately before cutting AND again after
     fitting, at that same spec, per the standing instruction below.
DECIDED 22/08/2026: Trev will use a torque wrench, ratchet head (not a preset
screwdriver). All rail fixings are M3 SHCS. X beam rail is tapped directly into
the aluminium beam wall (lower ceiling, ~1.0-1.5 N.m standard there to avoid
stripping the thread) - the Y rails run in full 3030 steel T-nuts, which support
more (M3 into steel is good for ~1.2-2.0 N.m before yield on a decent alloy
screw). SPEC SET: 2.0 N.m. Reason for the change from the original 1.0 N.m:
genuine ratchet-head torque wrenches do not click reliably below about 2 N.m -
the mechanism needs more leverage/spring travel than a low setting gives it, so
sub-2Nm work is normally done on a preset screwdriver instead. Since the Y rail
joint (steel T-nut) can safely take 2.0 N.m, moved the target up to the bottom
of a real ratchet's range rather than force the screwdriver tool. Repeatability
is what matters here, not hitting exactly 1.0 - a click wrench is just as
repeatable dialled to its minimum as anywhere else in range.
TOOL BOUGHT: CASOMAN 1/4" drive click torque wrench, 1-25 N.m variant, 72-tooth
head, dual direction, ~£19, Amazon UK. Chosen over the Venzo (2-15 N.m) because
the fine 72-tooth head needs only ~5deg of swing arc per click - the Venzo's
coarser ratchet needed more swing arc than the rail screw locations allow. 2.0
N.m sits well inside this wrench's range rather than at the floor, which is a
better spot for it too. Confirm the correct hex bit (2.5mm for M3 SHCS) is
fitted before use - not confirmed included in the base set.
CAVEAT: confirm the rail screws are alloy steel (12.9 or similar), not plain A2
stainless, before committing to 2.0 N.m - stainless M3 is usually derated lower
(~1.3-1.5 N.m) to avoid galling. If they're stainless, dial back to ~1.5 N.m and
accept it's below this wrench's rated minimum (a click wrench under-torques
rather than over-torques below its floor, which is the safer failure direction,
but log which number was actually used).
DO NOT DEVIATE FROM THE CHOSEN NUMBER once assembly starts.
SUPERSEDED 22/08/2026: 2.0 N.m above was a generic-table figure, PENDING. Trev
is instead going to characterise the real number directly - retorque the rail,
run input shaping, and use the resonance/ringing result (not a chart) to find
the torque this specific frame actually wants, since input shaping is what
caught the screws being over-tight in the first place. THE REAL SPEC IS NOT SET
YET. Do not cut or fit shims, and do not treat 2.0 N.m as final, until Trev

to the same number again after fitting.

UPDATE 23/08/2026 — THIS PLAN'S PREMISE IS WRONG. See sys/is.txt PIVOT section
for the full data. The input-shaping torque sweep this section anticipated was
run: original torque, 1.0 N.m, "by feel" twice, fully slack, and totally loose,
all n=3, full Y-position and belt-diagonal accelerometer sweeps. Result is
MONOTONIC with NO settled number - vibration falls all the way to zero clamp
force, which gave the best result of the whole series. There is no torque that
both locates the rail and avoids exciting it; every real clamping force
deflects the frame member, exactly the mechanism identified above, just now
confirmed dynamically as well as via the static bed-mesh twist.
Practical consequence: "wait for the number input shaping settles on" cannot be
satisfied as written, because no such settled number exists in this range.
Options going forward: (a) accept a minimum torque that reliably LOCATES the
rail (not zero - a rail that can slide is a separate real problem, already
found once this session) and treat the residual ringing/twist at that torque
as a characterised, compensated machine constant, the same way the X beam bow
above is accepted and meshed rather than eliminated; or (b) stiffen the joint
itself (backing plate behind the T-nut wall, or through-bolt instead of T-nut)
so clamping no longer deflects the extrusion, which would let a normal torque
be used without this trade-off.
NOT YET ANSWERED: whether this compliance is RIGHT-rail-specific (matching the
static shim fault, "do NOT shim the left rail" below) or a general property of
the T-nut joint on both sides — every torque test so far adjusted both rails
together. Isolate before finalising anything: adjust one rail at a time.

BEHIND WINS on twist. Difference map (bed shape cancels) is at the noise floor
almost everywhere - rms 0.011 - except at rear-right, directly over the shim,
which moved -0.025. Twist of the difference -0.028. Clean causal signature.
Mechanism: in front of the screw the shim is INSIDE the clamped span and lifts
properly; behind it, on the unclamped overhang, screw 10 pulls the rail back
down and the lift is only partly effective. In front is the stronger lift, and
since 0.5mm already over-corrects, stronger = worse.
=> 25mm of placement error is worth 0.028mm of twist. When cutting the real
   shims, placement accuracy matters at the SAME ORDER as the thickness steps.
   Get the hole positions right before worrying about foil thickness.

Twist series to date: +0.283 (no shim) -> +0.011/+0.024 (0.5 rear-right,
end-pinned) -> -0.037 (same, later) -> -0.058 (0.5 behind, fully fitted) ->
-0.086 (0.5 in front, fully fitted). A single 0.5mm point lift over-corrects
in the fully-fitted state. DO NOT read that as "section 3's 0.570 at #10 is
too big" - a single gauge bends the rail around one fulcrum, the graded stack
supports it continuously. Different load cases; the point-lift number does not
transfer. A 0.4mm bracketing run was considered and rejected for this reason -
it would tune a configuration that is about to be dismantled.

*** THE GANTRY RACK - 26/08/2026 LATE. THE REAL FAULT. READ THIS FIRST. ***
=======================================================================
*** THE RACK IS NOT A PRE-EXISTING FAULT. WE CREATED IT. ***
The machine was SQUARE WHEN BUILT and square when it went together. The rack
appeared ALONGSIDE THE BELT REMOVAL AND RETENSIONING - it is introduced by the
act of retensioning and by slackening the motor couplings, both of which move
the gantry while nothing is holding it square. Trev's point, and it changes
what this section is for: this is NOT "check for a hidden rack", it is
"YOUR TENSIONING PROCEDURE MAKES ONE unless you constrain the gantry".
Once created it dominated every other measurement on the machine. Squaring it
recovered 35% of motor authority in one step - more than every other change
made in three days put together.

    state              belt_check peak |PID|   authority used
    racked                227 / 240              89% / 94%
    squared               140 / 148              55% / 58%
    squared + tensioned   133 / 134              52% / 52%

WHY IT HID FOR SO LONG:
 - A RACK IS INVISIBLE TO THE MESH. It is an XY squareness error; the bed
   heightmap is blind to it. Twist read +0.001 (perfect) with the gantry
   badly out of square. NEVER take a good mesh as evidence of squareness.
 - IT MASQUERADES AS SLACK BELTS in anything that measures motor effort,
   because dragging a skewed gantry loads the motors exactly as a slack belt
   does. Hours were spent tensioning belts to fix it. Proof: tension was
   raised from 42 N to 64 N (+52%) and peak effort did not move at all
   (238.6 -> 239.6). The rack was swamping the tension signal completely.
 - HIGH TENSION MASKS IT. At 78 N the vibration numbers were the best ever
   recorded (8.4 um displacement) WHILE the motors sat at 89-94% authority.
   Those two results were reported separately and are the same finding:
   enough belt stiffness to hold a racked gantry rigidly buys low vibration
   at the price of nearly all the control range. Trev's read, and correct -
   that "best state ever" was a compensated state, not a healthy one.

*** SQUARE THE GANTRY MECHANICALLY BEFORE RESYNCING THE MOTOR COUPLINGS. ***
The coupling resync makes each motor's zero agree with WHEREVER THE GANTRY
HAPPENS TO BE. Do it racked and the motors accept the skew as correct -
zero position error, low idle PID - and the fault becomes the reference.
That is why the standstill fight kept "returning": each resync re-baselined
onto a worse skew. 366 -> 64 -> 16 while the geometry got worse.
LOW IDLE PID IS NOT A GEOMETRY CHECK. It is what a locked-in rack looks like.
Use belt_check for geometry; a rack shows there and cannot hide.

HOW THE RACK GETS INTRODUCED - IT WILL HAPPEN AGAIN:
 The tensioners are on the FRONT motor towers only, and each motor pair is a
 DIAGONAL - one front motor, one rear:
     X pair: 70.0 back-right + 71.0 front-left
     Y pair: 72.0 front-right + 73.0 back-left
 So tensioning changes the belt length between the two motors OF THE SAME
 PAIR. That forces a phase offset between motors commanded to identical
 positions, they fight, and the gantry racks because that is the compliant
 direction. Structural, not a mistake.

THE FIX FOR TENSIONING WITHOUT RACKING IT - ONE OPERATION, ONE POSITION:
 There are NO HARD STOPS on this machine (optical endstops only), so the
 gantry must be held square by a fixture. Trev used a pair of 1-2-3 blocks;
 printed matched spacers are being made for the front, where the grub screws
 are reachable. BOTH LEFT MOTORS (71.0 and 73.0) are the accessible pair and
 conveniently are one motor from each belt pair, which is exactly what is
 needed.
     1. gantry held square against the spacers
     2. slacken the couplings on 71.0 and 73.0   (pulleys now free)
     3. tension the belts - free pulleys rotate, NO phase offset is forced
     4. clamp the couplings, still held square
 Doing it this way means there is nothing to resync afterwards and no second
 visit that costs the reference.
 IF USING PRINTED SPACERS: they must be a MATCHED PAIR to better than the
 rack you are correcting. FDM tolerance (+/-0.1-0.2mm) is the same size as
 the error, so print slightly short, mic them, and SHIM THE SHORTER ONE with
 the foil stock to match. Print lying down so load runs along the layers.

WHAT EACH TOOL IS ACTUALLY GOOD FOR:
 SPECTROID / PLUCKING - only gives usable numbers when the belt is TOO TIGHT.
   Trev's observation and it is physically right: lower tension means lower
   frequency (toward 50Hz mains hum) and a slacker belt damps faster, so the
   signal weakens exactly as the noise gets relatively louder. THE METHOD'S
   USABLE RANGE STARTS ABOVE THE OPERATING RANGE. Readings of 79/82/124 on
   the same belt are the app latching onto different spans. Do not rely on it.

   which is BELOW what this machine needs. Its absolute numbers are

   belt-width tables. Needs a SQUARE gantry to mean anything.

   gets MORE sensitive as tension drops, i.e. it works exactly where plucking
   fails.  T = F * L / (4 * d).  On the 260mm span at d = 10mm:

BEST REMAINING EXPLANATION (fits everything, NOT proven): a MODE CROSSING.

severity set by speed (matches), disappearance at higher tension as the crossing
moves out of travel (matches), separate X and Y positions from different span
geometry (matches), and nothing physically wrong to find (matches).

RULES FOR THIS METHOD:
 - BOTH SIDES OF A FIXING MUST BE IDENTICAL. A fore/aft thickness difference
   does not average out - it pitches the rail about that screw. Cut each
   fixing's stack as a DOUBLE-LENGTH taped blank then cut it in half; the two
   halves are then identical by construction.
 - ORIENT FORE-AND-AFT along the rail, each piece full 12mm rail width. NOT
   left/right across the rail - that leaves two ~4mm strips with the rail
   bridging unsupported between them.
 - NOTHING RETAINS THEM. Unlike a slotted horseshoe on the shank, loose
   rectangles can slide out or be pushed aside as the rail settles. VERIFY
   BOTH PIECES ARE SEATED AT EVERY FIXING BEFORE TORQUING - a missing one is
   a 0.39 error at the worst place and reads exactly like a schedule failure.
 - Screw 5/6 overlap constraint no longer applies at ~8mm long.
 - Mechanically better than the notch: support is symmetric about the screw
   instead of material removed where clamping pressure peaks.

HISTORIC - SLOTTED METHOD, NO LONGER USED:
SHIMS MUST BE SLOTTED, NOT DRILLED. The screws cannot be withdrawn - they
thread into nuts that lose their position if removed. So each shim is a
horseshoe: 12mm (full rail width) x ~20-25mm, with a 3.2-3.5mm slot cut in
from a LONG edge to just past the centreline, sliding in perpendicular to the
rail from the side. Same side for all ten. Sliding in along the rail axis is
blocked by the neighbouring screw.
INSERTION: do NOT do one fastener at a time - lifting 0.57mm at the rear with
tightened neighbours 50mm away would bend the rail 0.57mm over 100mm. Slacken
ALL TEN about three turns (M3 x 0.5 pitch = ~1.5mm float, screws stay in their
nuts and keep the rail located laterally), slide all shims in, then torque
front to back at 0.30 N.m. Count turns out so nothing backs out of a nut.
Screws 5 and 6 are only 25mm apart - trim those two pieces to ~20mm long so
they cannot overlap or ride up on each other.

-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
4. OPERATING PROCEDURE
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
SOAK CRITERION - do NOT use frame temperature. temp0 flattens long before the
machine does; it read +0.03 C/min with the machine still 0.44mm from
equilibrium. The frame is only partly insulated, so different members have
different time constants and no single sensor can represent it.
Use the Z DATUM instead - it integrates the whole loop. Probe bed centre with
G30 S-1 every 3 min and wait for it to stop moving.
NOTE THE DATUM OVERSHOOTS AND COMES BACK: it peaked 10 min before reversing at
~2 microns/min during the 60C soak. Bed and Z system warm first (datum up), then
the towers catch up (datum down). A criterion that waits for "stopped changing"
will fire twice - once near the peak, once for real.

MESH: the ALPS cannot complete a large mesh in a hot chamber - it latches
"probe already triggered" after ~21-25 points. Known behaviour of this probe.
Use 4x4 hot. A cold 7x7 is fine.

SYNTHESISED HOT MESH: fine 7x7 cold, coarse 4x4 hot, subsample the cold map at
indices 0,2,4,6 in both axes (the 4x4 points are an exact subset), take the
difference, bilinear-interpolate up to 7x7, add to the cold map, recall with
G29 S1. Re-datum hot regardless - G29 S1 restores shape, not Z0.
The recalled map dies if the FR4 is lifted. Clip or magnetically hold it.

5. TRAPS - ALL OF THESE COST HOURS
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- M558 SILENTLY WIPES THE G31 TRIGGER HEIGHT. Any runtime M558 reset it from
  -0.030 to an old 0.700 placeholder while config.g still read -0.030. Every map
  taken in that window was displaced 0.73mm. ALWAYS re-issue G31 after M558, in
  that order. Verify: M409 K"sensors.probes[0]" -> triggerHeight must be -0.030.
- G30 S-1 reports the RAW trigger height; G29 stores it with G31 Z already
  subtracted. Comparing the two directly looks like a 0.7mm fault and is not one.
- RRF heightmap row 0 = Ymin = FRONT.
- Heightmap positive = bed reads HIGH = the gantry sits LOW there.
- bed.g's final centre G30 can fail silently and leave the datum unset, which
  displaces a whole map while reporting success. The result check is now in
  bed.g - keep it.
- PHYSICAL E-STOP HELD IN = the board is held in boot and reports state
  "starting", not "idle". That is NOT a DCS fault - it is the lockout working.
  M999 with the E-stop still depressed does not release it; it just quiets the
  fans. Release the button before expecting the machine to come back.
  This is the safest state for working in the belt path - hardware lockout,
  not software (26/08/2026).
- DO NOT DRIVE THIS MACHINE WITH ONE "CodeConsole -c" PER COMMAND. Each
  invocation opens a fresh socket to DCS, allocates a code channel and tears it
  down. Dozens in tight loops correlate with the unexplained M112 halts, and the
  event log caught the mechanism: "Transfer timeout waiting for TfrRdy (waited
  504ms, 0 glitches)" - the Duet too busy to answer, not electrical noise.
  Halts only ever occurred during direct machine control, never in normal use.
  Batch commands down ONE connection instead:
      printf 'M114\nM409 K"state.status"\n' | CodeConsole
  SbcTransferTimeout also raised 500 -> 2000ms in /opt/dsf/conf/config.json
  (bak: config.json.bak-timeout). Needs a duetcontrolserver restart, not M999.
- M929 event logging is now enabled (eventlog.txt, on the SD card, survives
  reboots). Added after repeated unexplained M112 halts with no persistent log.
- Rebooting the Pi does NOT reset the Duet - it has its own power, and config.g
  does not re-run. Use M999.
- chamber_abort.g sets chamberSP=0, which makes chamber_heat.g's wait condition
  immediately false, so it exits the normal way and never clears chamberAbort.
  Still unfixed.
- daemon.g line 49: `if { var.plcRegs != null }` throws "unknown variable" when
  the Modbus read fails, aborting the file. Needs exists() guarding it.

THE 90 DEGREE ROTATION - MACHINE vs STONE
A quarter turn swaps the stone's two curvature axes while the machine's share
stays put. bowY fell 0.1188 and bowX rose 0.1192 - equal and opposite to 0.4um,
which is the signature. Solving both ways round:
   stone astigmatism   bowY-bowX = 0.119   (0.3255-0.2067, and 0.0873-(-0.0319))
   machine astigmatism bowY-bowX = 0.238   (0.3255-0.0873, and 0.2067-(-0.0319))
Two independent derivations agreeing to 1um. SO THE SADDLE IS ROUGHLY TWO-THIRDS
MACHINE, ONE-THIRD STONE. The 0.119 stone figure matches this file's existing
0.114mm granite astigmatism. The 0.238 machine figure matches section 6's
already-recorded "both rails crown ~0.2mm common mode" - that crown IS the
machine's bowY, arrived at by a completely independent route.

THE MACHINE SHARE IS MOUNTING-INDEPENDENT. bed.g probes X398 Y16, X201 Y380,
X2 Y16, which track the M671 actuator geometry and therefore the three points
the slab rests on whatever holds it there. The item 8 mounting decision does not
change it, and 0.238 carries across to the replacement slab.

TRAVEL SPEED RAISED TO 200 mm/s, 30/08/2026 - NO EFFECT ON THE MEASUREMENT

   dropped from the fit made twist WORSE, not better (datum range 0.0032 ->
   0.0101). Twist is a least-squares term over all 49 points; no two points
   dominate it.
 - GRID DENSITY. 4x4 and 7x7 agree on twist to 0.001.
 - PROBE / MEASUREMENT. rms has held 0.054-0.059 through every configuration,
   machine position and hardware change since the sheet was bolted.

WHAT IS NOT RULED OUT: the bed carrier or Z arms (an arm that is TILTED, or a
carrier twisted between the three arms, is non-planar and survives G32), and
slab seating on its three supports. The timing favours the first - the rear
motor dropping 100mm and being wound back by hand is the only disturbance in
the window big enough to leave a set. Two tests that need no granite: lift the
slab and set it down, three meshes (moves = seating); or indicator against the
BED FRAME at the three arm positions while jogging Z (tells you if the carrier
itself is twisted, independent of what sits on it).

SUPPORT GEOMETRY - WORTH KNOWING BEFORE THE GRANITE IS DRILLED
M671 puts the supports at (424.75,-8.75) front-right, (201,415) rear, and
(-22.75,-8.75) front-left. The rear support is a SINGLE POINT AT CENTRE, so the
support triangle narrows to a 32mm span by Y384 and the rear corners of the
probe grid overhang it by 175mm. 24 of the 49 grid points sit OUTSIDE the
triangle. This shows in per-point repeatability: rear-right repeats to ~19um
against 3-8um along the front row, matching the support asymmetry exactly.
Stiffness sum says it is NOT the granite bending - 20mm granite, 175mm overhang
needs ~50N to deflect 30um, and no strain-gauge probe triggers near that.

THERMAL MOVEMENT AT THE 8-HOLE PATTERN (440x440 slab, holes 7.5mm in from the
edge, so 425mm corner-to-corner, 300.5mm radial from centre):
   granite a    dT40 (60C)    dT55 (75C)      per axis at dT40
     6e-6        0.206mm       0.283mm          0.145mm
     8e-6        0.182mm       0.250mm          0.128mm
    11e-6        0.145mm       0.200mm          0.103mm
Movement is RADIAL - each corner moves diagonally outward, so a fastener there
flexes in both axes at once. The 5mm holes on M3 hardware give 1mm of radial
clearance, a 4x margin, so the geometry is covered by clearance alone and no
flexure length is required. THE REMAINING VARIABLE IS FRICTION: a washer
clamped hard on granite resists sliding, and the failure mode is STICK-SLIP -
holds, load builds, releases suddenly, slab lands somewhere slightly different.
Nip rather than torque, and something low-friction under the head.
