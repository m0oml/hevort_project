MEASUREMENT DISCIPLINE - every one of these has cost real time:  
 - COMPARE STATES IN DISPLACEMENT, NOT ACCELERATION RMS.  
   d = a * 9.81 / (2*pi*f)^2. Acceleration rms weights harmless 200Hz content  
   the same as damaging 40Hz content. Hours went into chasing a "35% worse  
   than benchmark" gap that did not exist once converted.  
 - CHECK BELT TENSION BEFORE ANY COMPARISON. is_diag.g isolates one belt pair  
   at a time and is directly exposed to a tension imbalance - an UNCHANGED  
   machine gave 1.03x / 1.19x / 1.02x purely from slack belts, and that  
   artefact was read as a rail asymmetry.  
 - Single captures have ~15% run-to-run scatter. At least THREE runs before  
   trusting any modest effect.  
 - The accelerometer is config.g line 61, COMMENTED OUT - uncomment it for a  
   session. There is NO macros/accel_setup.g, despite earlier notes saying so.  
 - Do NOT write new sweep macros. Use macros/Frame/ (is_xpos.g, is_diag.g,  
   is_x390_frontrear.g, belt_check.g) or the plugin, whose 135Hz ceiling is  
   deliberate.  
 - is_xpos.g / is_diag.g write FIXED filenames and silently overwrite. Archive  
   to /home/trev/hevort_project/survey_data/ BEFORE re-running.  
 - Velocity sweeps distinguish structural modes (frequency does not scale with  
   velocity) from forced modes (belt tooth-passing, bearing ball-pass).  
 - M956 accepts S3000 and A1 ("activate just before the next move") for exact  
   position mapping - use that for localisation work, not S1000 A0.

14. FILAMENT HANDLING — UNDECIDED  
---------------------------------------------------------------------------  
Three options, no decision. IO cost is the deciding constraint: only IO0 and  
IO4 are free, with IO3 reserved for a runout sensor.  

 a. Mellow filament buffer board. Dedicated onboard MCU with its own  
    closed-loop buffer control. IO cost 1 (runout on IO3), rising to 3 if  
    FEED/RETRACT button-to-host signalling is also wired — which would consume  
    every remaining free IO.  

 b. Duet rotating-magnet filament monitor. Jam and under-extrusion detection  
    only; does NOT address feed-path drag, which is the actual problem.  
    IO cost 1 (fits IO4).  

 c. DIY buffer. Mobius 4 (Voron) frame-mounted geared extruder as the buffer  
    feed motor, driven via RRF's M567 tool mixing-ratio — NOT M584  
    axis-bonding. Mixing ratios apply per extruder drive independently, each  
    keeping its own M92, so there is no forced-identical-drivetrain  
    constraint. Concept ported from Klipper/Box Turtle AFC's bang-bang  
    gear-ratio correction: a baseline mix ratio absorbs any gross drivetrain  
    mismatch, and two limit switches on the buffer mechanism trigger a small  
    swing above and below baseline (e.g. 0.95/1.05, or an offset from any  
    baseline such as 1.25/1.35) to correct drift.  
    IO cost 3 (2x buffer limit switches + 1x runout) — fits exactly in  
    IO0/IO3/IO4 with nothing spare. Conceptual only; mechanism not sized or  
    built.

FRAME GEOMETRY AND VIBRATION - SETTLED 23-26/08/2026

Three days of work, several wrong turns, and one procedure change that matters  
more than all the rest. Full detail in ~/hevort_project/is.txt and
~/hevort_project/frame_survey_20260821.txt on the Pi (both moved out of sys/

REAR-RIGHT Y RAIL SHIM - the machine's one shimmable geometry fault. 0.5mm at  
the rear-right fastener. Confirmed by two independent methods:  
   static   cold 4x4 mesh corner twist    0.283mm -> 0.011mm  
   dynamic  127Hz band at X=390           2.25x rear-LOUDER -> 0.63x rear-QUIETER  
PERMANENT SHIM FITTED 29/08/2026, replacing the temporary slip gauge at the  
same ~0.5mm. VERIFIED SOUND 30/08/2026 by six bare-granite 7x7s: twist +0.006  
and +0.016 against ~0.28 before the shim. The earlier plan to match the  
benchmark map is STRUCK - that map was taken through the loose FR4, which  
moves twist by ~0.24mm on its own. NOTE the static 4x4 evidence above  
(0.283 -> 0.011) was also measured through the sheet and is contaminated; the  
DYNAMIC 127Hz result is the shim's real proof. See frame_survey_20260821.txt.  

FASTENER TORQUE: 0.5 N.m, ALL RAILS, set 29/08/2026 with the torque screwdriver.  
This is the documented, repeatable spec the rail investigation needed and never  
had. USE THE SAME FIGURE for the verification mesh and for final assembly -  
retorquing moves the twist by tens of microns per event, so changing it silently  
invalidates the shim. For scale: 1.0 N.m was tested directly and was WAY too  
tight, and the 2.0 N.m-floor ratchet bought early on cannot reach 0.5 at all.  

THE RACKED GANTRY - the big one, and WE CREATED IT. The machine was square when  
built. The rack appeared alongside belt removal and retensioning, because both  
retensioning and slackening the motor couplings move the gantry while nothing  
holds it square. Squaring it took belt_check peak effort from 227/240 to  
133/134 - about 35% of motor authority recovered in a single step, more than  
every other change of those three days combined.  
 - THE MESH IS BLIND TO IT. A rack is an XY squareness error; twist read +0.001  
   (essentially perfect) with the gantry badly out of square.  
 - IT MASQUERADES AS SLACK BELTS. Raising tension 42 -> 64 N (+52%) moved peak  
   effort not at all, 238.6 -> 239.6. Hours went into tensioning belts to fix  
   what was actually a geometry fault.  
 - HIGH TENSION MASKS IT. At 78 N the vibration numbers were the best ever  
   measured while the motors sat at 89-94% authority. Both are the same finding:  
   belt stiffness holding a racked gantry rigidly buys quiet at the cost of  
   nearly all the control range. That "best state ever" was compensated, not  
   healthy.  

BELT TENSION - ~40 N per belt, set by PLUCK, T = 4L^2 f^2 mu (85/88 Hz on a  
260mm span). The RC2 meter tops out at 29.4 N and is therefore useless on this  
machine. The earlier 53-78 N figures were sized for a racked gantry - do not  
use them.  

MOTOR COUPLING RESYNC - the four closed-loop motors fight each other at  
standstill after any belt refit, burning 366 units of PID authority against a  
healthy ~70. This is a REASSEMBLY ARTEFACT and it RECURS.  
 - SQUARE THE GANTRY FIRST. The resync makes each motor's zero agree with  
   wherever the gantry currently is, so doing it racked makes the skew the  
   reference - which is why the fight kept "returning" (366 -> 64 -> 16, each  
   resync re-baselining onto a worse skew). LOW IDLE PID IS NOT A GEOMETRY  
   CHECK.  
 - Motors ENGAGED AND HOLDING, slacken one coupling at a time on the plain  
   motor shaft, let the controller drive its own error to zero, re-clamp. Do  
   NOT disable the motors to re-clamp - that locks in the mechanical rest  
   position and the fight comes straight back.  

BEST TENSIONING PROCEDURE. Tensioners are on the FRONT towers only and each  
pair is a diagonal (X = 70 back-right + 71 front-left; Y = 72 front-right +  
73 back-left), so tensioning changes belt length BETWEEN a pair and forces a  
phase offset. Hold square against a fixture, slacken the couplings on 71 and 73  
(both left, one per pair), tension with those pulleys free, then clamp. One  
operation, one position, no resync needed afterwards.  

REFERENCE STATE, SQUARE: plucked 85/88 Hz = 37/40 N, RC2 2.8, belt_check  
133.4/133.9, standstill 70, silent. Mean displacement 8.4um across X.  

RULED OUT WITH EVIDENCE - DO NOT RE-OPEN. Rails and blocks are FINE (hand-run  
end to end with the belts off and under deliberate racking, inspected section  
by section) - do not order replacements. Also excluded: the umbilical and  
sensor lead, anything rotating (it would repeat every ~40mm), and open versus  
closed loop (identical). TORQUE was never the variable - there is no sweet  
spot, vibration falls monotonically to zero clamp force, because clamping a  
straight rail against an untrue surface twists the carriage.

THERMAL BEHAVIOUR - MEASURED 29/08/2026  
=======================================================================================================  
The Z datum moves with CHAMBER temperature, and it is the single biggest source  
of error in a hot mesh. Measured by repeated G30 S-1 at bed centre with the  
hotend PINNED AT 150C throughout, so nozzle growth is excluded by construction.  

   datum vs chamber      10.7 um per degC, R^2 = 0.93  
   during a 40->60C climb   307 um of datum movement  
   once chamber settled      23 um band, residual drift 1.2 um/min  

THE HOTEND IS NOT THE DRIVER. It sat at 150.0 +/-0.2C while the datum moved  
307um. Holding the hotend at temperature does NOT stabilise the datum.  

10.7 um/degC sits close to the documented X beam bimetallic bow of -11.7 um/K  
(frame_survey_20260821.txt) - two independent methods, ~9% apart. This is very  
likely that same already-characterised frame expansion showing up in Z.  

WHAT THIS MEANS IN PRACTICE  
 - Never mesh or tram while the chamber is still MOVING. At 10.7 um/degC even  
   5C of residual drift smears 50um across the map, comparable to real bed  
   features.  
 - The datum must be set at the same chamber temperature the mesh is taken at.  
 - There is NO long soak needed. It settles within a minute or two of the  
   chamber reaching setpoint - the requirement is stability, not waiting.  
 - Residual 1.2 um/min still applies: a 10 minute 49-point mesh carries ~12um  
   of drift as a gentle gradient.  

THE CHAMBER SENSOR READS AIR, AND AIR CAN LIE ABOUT THE STRUCTURE  
Opening the door to cool faster cools the AIR, not the frame or slab. Measured  
29/08/2026: with the door open the air read 35.7C; closing it, the air CLIMBED  
to 37.5C in five minutes as the mass fed heat back in. A threshold alone is  
therefore not a safe trigger for meshing - the air must be steady WITH THE DOOR  
SHUT. The rebound on closing the door is a free proxy for the structure, which  
has no sensor of its own. Passive cooldown time constant is ~146 minutes.  

HOT vs COLD MESH, 29/08/2026 - THE FRONT-TO-REAR TILT IS LARGELY THERMAL  
                        pts     mean  deviation   range    twist  front-rear  
  HOT  chamber60/hotend150  49  -0.1901   0.1844  0.7180  +0.0680    -0.3581

GOTCHAS THAT KEEP BITING  
=======================================================================================================  
Firmware:  
- M569 D-parameter: 2=spreadCycle, 3=stealthChop, 4=closed loop, 5=assisted  
  open loop. D3 is NOT assisted open loop.  
- M569.1 must precede M569 D for a given driver. M569.1 WITH parameters drops  
  drivers D4->D2; re-home to restore closed loop.  
- M569.7 (not M569.2) is brake control in RRF 3.3+.  
- M201 applies at queue time, not immediately — M400 after setting it.  
- M917 cannot take per-motor colon-syntax lists. M913 cannot target individual  
  motors within a multi-driver axis.  
- M671 must be in config.g before G32 will work.  
- G1 without H defaults to H0, which applies axis limits and refuses unhomed  
  axes. Homing back-off moves need H2. But G1 H2 on CoreXY bypasses the  
  kinematic transform and drives motors individually — do not use it for  
  CoreXY back-off moves. Z H2 is fine.  
- On CoreXY, home axes SEQUENTIALLY.  
- M593 in RRF 3.6 is GLOBAL only, no axis parameter.  
- M42 with no S parameter errors in RRF 3.6 (it does not query state).  
- Object model path for GPIO output state is state.gpOut, not boards[0].gpOut.  
- M906 idle timeout reduces current only — it does NOT engage brakes. Brakes  
  engage only on explicit M18/M84.  
- M106 accepts a sensor LIST on H and uses the highest reading.  
- vars.g needs exists() guards on all global declarations.  
- RRF 3.6.x has no bitwise operators — use mod arithmetic.  
- RRF 3.7.0: M575 P2 became P3. Already applied in config.g and daemon.g.  
- RRF 3.7.0: a FAILED M261.1 leaks its V variable, permanently poisoning every  
  later iteration of a while loop, and DSF escalates the error to a full  
  EMERGENCY STOP. exists() guards were tried and do NOT prevent it - an  
  earlier note here claiming daemon.g carries an exists()/break guard was  
  WRONG, corrected 29/08/2026. The fix is daemon.g's single-pass loop block;  
  do not 'fix' it to while true. See DAEMON.G SINGLE-PASS LOOP in  
  project-long.txt and ~/duet_bug_report_20260829.txt.  
  UPSTREAM - ROOT CAUSE FOUND AND FIXED BY DUET3D, from Trev's own bug  
  report. Commit 363e8743, 31/08/2026, chrishamm, "Fixed tracking of local  
  variables from M260.1/M261.1". Per Trev it was fixed within about 30  
  minutes of the report being received. Forum thread:  
  https://forum.duet3d.com/topic/39425/3.7.0-beta.3-modbus-daemon.g-issues/4  
  Four fixes in it. TWO ARE OUR EXACT FAILURE, and both surface as  
  "unknown variable 'plcRegs'":  
    - result variables did not mark their code block as having local  
      variables, so the BLOCK WAS CLOSED WITHOUT WAITING for codes that  
      still referenced them - i.e. plcRegs was torn down while read-ahead  
      was still parsing the line that uses it. That is the race, and it is  
      why the fault is intermittent rather than every-time;  
    - a failed M260.1/M261.1 still REGISTERED its result variable, so  
      deleting it later reported an unknown variable.  
  A third fix - variables declared before any code block was opened are now  
  tracked instead of only warned about - RETIRES THE SINGLE-PASS LOOP  
  WORKAROUND once we are on a build that has it. M261.1 ... V"plcRegs" will  
  then be legal at file top level.  
  WE NOW HAVE IT - CONFIRMED 08/09/2026. Commit 363e8743 (31/08/2026) is an  
  ancestor of tag v3.7.0-rc.1: the GitHub compare API reports behind_by 0,  
  ahead_by 7. The rc.1 release notes also list all three bug fixes verbatim  
  under "Bug fixes". We upgraded beta.3 -> rc.1 on 08/09/2026.  
  KEEP THE SINGLE-PASS LOOP ANYWAY - see the next paragraph. The declaration  
  bug being fixed does NOT make a top-level M261.1 safe here, and we have not  
  tested one, deliberately.  
  WHAT THE COMMIT DOES NOT CHANGE: an expression error raised during  
  read-ahead still escalates to a full EMERGENCY STOP. That policy stands,  
  so the same class of halt stays reachable by other means.  
- Brake configurations must be bench-tested in isolation (motors decoupled,  
  timing verified) before live reload under load. Hard rule after the Z brake  
  spring damage incident.  

Hardware / electrical:  
- DC 0V is WHITE H07V-K. Blue is reserved for AC neutral only, sleeve is the  
  sole identification, no heatshrink bands.  
- Z probe terminals are REVERSED from the obvious assumption: T10 = io6.out  
  (ENABLE), T11 = io6.in (SIGNAL). Labelled at the block.  
- Duet IO header pin order: 1=3.3V, 2=io.in, 3=GND, 4=io.out, 5=5V_EXT.  
  io.in and io.out are NOT adjacent.  
- Do not plug an M23CL into an already-live TDB — blows the motor's internal  
  2A fuse, not user-replaceable on pre-v1.2 units.

- CF-filled nylon carries non-negligible conductivity and can corrupt 4-wire  
  Pt100 sense circuits via leakage across exposed solder joints.  
- Acceleration ceiling is governed by frame resonance, not motor torque.  
  Beam stiffness scales as 1/L^3 — span is the dominant variable.  
- RETENSIONING THE BELTS RACKS THE GANTRY unless it is physically held square.  
  The rack is invisible to the mesh and reads as slack belts. See FRAME  
  GEOMETRY AND VIBRATION.  
- The RC2 belt meter tops out at 29.4 N, below this machine's ~40 N working  
  tension. Pluck the belt and use T = 4L^2 f^2 mu instead.  
- The chamber sensor reads AIR. Opening the door decouples it from the frame and  
  slab, so a cold reading can sit on top of a warm structure. Judge readiness by  
  the air being STEADY with the door SHUT, never by the number alone.  
- The `duet` client returns from a long macro (mesh.g) roughly 90s BEFORE the  
  machine finishes it, printing "The operation was canceled". Do not archive  
  heightmap.csv on that return - you will copy the PREVIOUS map. Wait for the  
  "[MESH] saved" line in the journal.  
- If a defect looks mechanical and permanent, RE-TEST AFTER A FEW HUNDRED  
  PASSES before spending money. A position-locked, clamp-immune, non-periodic  
  signature was twice read as a brinelled rail; it was belt tension both times.  
- This Waveshare display's 15-pin FFC cables have contacts on the SAME face at  
  both ends, NOT the mirrored type that generic Pi and Waveshare guides assume.  
  Orientation is consistent at both connectors, confirmed by inspection.

DO NOT REOPEN (settled)  
=======================================================================================================  
- CF vs aluminium X beam; dual MGN9 rail sandwich. M23CL rotor inertia  
  dominates system dynamics, so beam mass arguments are moot.  
- Flow switch removal — accepted risk, deliberate, recorded.  
- Encoder calibration (M569.6 V2) — persists in motor flash, does not need  
  repeating.  
- Trigger renumbering to T0/T1 — impossible. RRF hardcodes trigger 0 to M112  
  and trigger 1 to M25; those files are silently ignored. Staying on T2/T3.  
- Mellow left-rear shim documentation error — documentation fault, not a build  
  fault. Belt plane confirmed correct visually.  
- 5A TDB fuses, not 3A.  
- Sealed double-glazed unit and polycarbonate primary pane, both rejected.  
- 9mm->12mm shim stacks (2mm out per position). Belt tooth-count parity is  
  irrelevant; matched tension is what matters.  
- 4mm FR4, not 5mm or 3.2mm.  
- Inrush: T2 is permanently powered, no limiter required.  
- global.awdDriveMode — a switchable AWD drive mode set from a DWC macro.  
  Trev's own idea, DROPPED 29/08/2026: closed loop is working well, so there is  
  nothing to switch between and the complexity buys nothing. The hardcoded  
  M569 D2/D4 pairs in homex.g, homey.g and homeall.g stay exactly as they are.  
  Do not re-propose it.  
- No M501, no config-override.g. config.g is HAND-EDITED and that is the  
  deliberate, preferred workflow — do not propose M500/M501 or an override  
  file. Consequence to remember, not a fault: an autotune result (M307, and  
  anything else M500 would normally persist) lives in RAM only, so transcribe  
  it into config.g by hand before the next M999 or power cycle.
