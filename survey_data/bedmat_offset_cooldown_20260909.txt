QUESTION

  offset or a slope/beta error?

  S2 = 100k NTC, generic B3950 curve, on temp2. CONTROL SENSOR for heater 0
       and carries the only M143 limit (110C).
  S5 = bed slab Pt100, 4-wire, MAX31865 on spi.cs1. Monitor only.

  Trev's position, and the reason for the test: a 4-wire Pt100 on a proper
  amplifier is trusted over a generic Chinese NTC.

  SENSOR GRADES - corrected 10/09/2026. Do NOT treat these as equal:
    S5 slab    4-wire Pt100 on MAX31865   reference grade
    S4 ElecBay WIKA 4-wire Pt100 on MAX31865   reference grade
    chamberPV  weighted average of a WIKA 4-wire Pt100 and two 3-wire
               Pt100, via the S7-1200 / SM1231 and Modbus. Good grade and

    S0 hotend  INEXPENSIVE 2-WIRE Pt1000  NOT a reference. Lead resistance

               the stop condition and the dT column - it carries no weight
               as corroboration.
    S2 mat     generic 100k NTC, B3950 only, C=0

METHOD
  The mat is bonded to the granite with Dowsil 736, so with the heater off
  the two are in intimate thermal contact. Any genuine gradient decays as
  the assembly approaches ambient; the surviving difference is sensor error.
  Log mat, slab and the difference every 30s from hot to ambient and beyond.

  M308 correction available (RRF 3.5+):
      adjustedReading = (rawReading * (1.0 + V)) + U
    delta constant across the range  -> fixed offset, correct with U
    delta varies with temperature    -> beta/slope error, needs V
  Both U and V are currently 0 on S2 (config.g line 84).

  Mat surface by IR : 45.4 C

THIS CUTS AGAINST THE OFFSET HYPOTHESIS. An independent instrument agrees
with S2, not with the slab RTD. If S2 is right at 45C then the ~2.8C delta
is a REAL gradient, not sensor error, and no U correction is warranted.

The mat is RED silicone. Colour in the visible has little bearing on
long-wave IR emissivity for polymers - silicone rubber sits at ~0.95
regardless, close to the 805i's fixed value, so this reading should be sound.

UNRESOLVED CONFLICT
  09/09 09:44, cold, 12h idle: mat 24.01 vs slab 21.08, same ~2.9 delta —
  at equilibrium a real gradient should not exist. But the garage heating
  was on and the shutter had been open, and the granite lags room air by
  hours while the thin mat tracks it. So that morning reading may not have
  been at equilibrium either, which would reconcile the two.

  Note also the morning clusters do NOT split by sensor type:
     mat NTC 24.01 + ElecBay Pt100 24.33  |  hotend Pt1000 21.55 + slab Pt100 21.08
  Mixed types in both groups argues spatial/real over calibration.

DECISIVE MEASUREMENT STILL NEEDED
  IR the EXPOSED GRANITE, ideally close to the RTD pocket, at the same
  moment as a logged sample.
    reads ~42.4 -> RTD is right, delta is a real gradient, no correction
    reads ~45   -> RTD reads low, and the correction belongs on S5 not S2
  Caveat: polished granite is a poorer IR target than the silicone
  (lower and more variable emissivity, more reflective), so treat that
  reading as weaker evidence than the mat one.

  Also worth knowing: the RTD sits in a pocket in the SIDE of the slab, so
  it senses the edge. During cooldown the edge loses heat on more faces
  than the centre, so slab-edge < slab-centre is physically expected — a
  further reason the delta may be real rather than instrumental.

  NOTE RRF stores/reports offsetAdj as -2.9 (rounds the third digit).

  WHAT THIS DOES NOT ESTABLISH:
   - Validity above room temperature. Every setpoint now runs 2.91C hotter
     in reality than the same number did on 09/09, including anything
     compared against that day's M307 model, which was tuned UNCORRECTED.
   - The shape of the error across the range. The cooldown delta is sensor
     error PLUS a real gradient which cannot be separated without
     equilibrium points at temperature. The U-shaped curve seen during
     cooldown (min 2.39 at 37C) is real in the data but must NOT be
     attributed to the sensor.
   - The Testo 805i IR reading (45.4C on the mat at 45C) is NOT decisive:
     that instrument class is typically +-2C, comparable to the effect.

  TO GO FURTHER: hold the bed at a setpoint until FULLY soaked (hours),
  compare at equilibrium, repeat at two or three temperatures. Only
  gradient-free points can characterise the curve.
